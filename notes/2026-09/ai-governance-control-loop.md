# AI Governance Control Loop

![Infographic](/img/notes/2026-09/ai-governance-control-loop.png)

## Summary
The AI governance control loop turns intent into governed action: pressure-test risks, grant conditional permission, deploy with controls, watch real-world behavior, step in when conditions shift, and fold lessons back into the next decision cycle.

## Key points
- Governance begins with intent and success metrics, then pressure-tests potential harms, affected parties, and impact.
- Approval is conditional: access and usage limits, explicit guardrails, and review dates make permission revocable rather than a blank cheque.
- Going live includes technical controls, human oversight, and escalation paths; continuous monitoring watches for drift, misuse, anomalies, feedback, and incidents.
- The loop closes through predefined interventions (continue, restrict, or stop) and by feeding lessons into updated policies, controls, models, and processes.
- Five supporting pillars are named: ownership, risk-matched rigor, human oversight, evidence, and continuous improvement.

## Why I saved this
A compact operating model for treating AI governance as a repeating decision loop rather than a one-time approval checklist.

## OCR text

```text
The AI Governance Control Loop

Where Every Decision Gets Made and Remade.

Intent — What are we trying to do? Define the use case and the outcome we want.
Set Direction: define business objective; identify value and scope; align on success metrics.

Pressure-Test — What could go wrong? Surface the risks and who they touch.
Assess Risks: identify potential harms; assess affected parties; evaluate likelihood and impact.

Grant — Who approves, and on what terms? Permission with conditions, not a blank cheque.
Set Guardrails: define approval process; set access and usage limits; attach conditions and review dates.

Go Live — Deploy with controls. Ship with guardrails and a human in the loop.
Deploy Responsibly: enable technical controls; keep a human in the loop; ensure clear escalation paths.

Watch — Is it still behaving? Monitor against expected behavior in the real world.
Monitor Continuously: track behavior and performance; monitor for drift, misuse and anomalies; review user feedback and incidents.

Step In — Act the moment it shifts. Continue, restrict, or stop.
Take Action: trigger predefined responses; adjust permissions or scope; pause or stop if needed.

Fold Back — What did we learn? Feed the lesson into the next cycle.
Improve and Adapt: capture what changed; update policies and controls; refine models and processes.

The 5 Pillars of Strong AI Governance: own it by name (no orphaned systems, clear accountability); match rigor to risk (heavier controls where stakes are higher); keep humans in the call (oversight at decisions that matter); prove it with evidence (every action logged and reviewable); improve every cycle (feed each lesson into the next round).
```
