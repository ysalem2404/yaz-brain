# Governance & Compliance Landscape

![Infographic](/img/notes/2026-09/governance-compliance-landscape.png)

## Summary
A governance-and-compliance landscape maps AI controls from laws and standards through content safety, identity, privacy, auditability, model risk, and red-teaming assurance.

## Key points
- The standards layer references the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, OECD AI Principles, and GDPR.
- Content-safety controls span input filtering for prompt injection, jailbreak detection, and topic allow-lists, plus output toxicity screening, grounding checks, PII redaction, and data or abuse limits.
- Identity and access separates human access controls such as MFA, role-based access, and least-privilege review from agent identity controls such as non-human authentication, scoped API keys, tool-use permissions, and least-privilege access.
- Data privacy covers classification and tagging, PII detection and masking, retention and deletion, regional residency, tenant isolation, and third-party data agreements.
- Audit and lineage add prompt and response logging, model-version tracking, dataset lineage, decision records, approval workflows, and immutable audit trails.
- Model-risk management includes use-case risk tiering, impact assessment, bias and fairness assessment, model cards, human-in-the-loop review, performance and drift monitoring, and a kill switch; the lower red-teaming and assurance section is partially cropped in the capture.

## Why I saved this
A useful crosswalk for explaining that trustworthy AI is a control system spanning policy, runtime safeguards, identity, data, evidence, risk review, and assurance—not a single compliance checkbox.
