# Data Governance for Leaders Scaling AI

## Summary

Clare Kitching's leadership-focused data governance primer defines governance as practices ensuring data is accurate, secure, and used responsibly, walking through a four-step chain (Data Governance → Trusted Data → Responsible AI → Business Value), explaining 'why now' (AI scaling without governance stalls pilots, trust is critical, regulation is tightening), what governance includes (ownership/accountability, data quality/fitness, metadata/lineage, access/privacy/security controls, risk/compliance monitoring), what leaders should do (treat governance as AI infrastructure not just compliance, focus on data that matters most, assign clear owners, measure quality/usage/control effectiveness), questions to ask, and terms to know (data owner, data steward, data quality, metadata).

## Key Points

- Four-step value chain: Data Governance → Trusted Data → Responsible AI → Business Value, framing governance as the foundation enabling downstream AI value
- 'Why Now' section ties governance urgency to three forces: AI scaling (pilots stall without it), trust (governance improves confidence in AI outputs), and tightening regulation (fines/reputational risk)
- Explicit leader action item: 'Treat governance as AI infrastructure, not just compliance' — reframing governance from a checkbox to a technical prerequisite
- Four 'questions to ask' give leaders a ready checklist: which decisions/use cases rely on this data, who owns it, can we trust its quality/lineage/access controls, what risks could poor governance create
- This is a companion piece to Clare Kitching's other 'Data Governance Explained to a Non-Tech Exec' infographic captured later in this batch — same terms-to-know glossary
