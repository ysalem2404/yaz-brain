# 3 Roles of AI Every Leader Should Understand: Traditional, Generative, Agentic

## Summary

Clare Kitching's three-column comparison profiles Traditional AI ('The Analyst' — studies data, predicts outcomes; high maturity; risks: outdated data, biased inputs, data leakage), Generative AI ('The Creator' — produces new content/ideas/code; medium maturity; risks: can invent facts, leak sensitive info, be manipulated by prompts), and Agentic AI ('The Worker' — takes action across systems to complete tasks; early maturity; risks: errors can cascade, needs strict guardrails/approvals/rollback controls). Each column covers what it does, where it shines, what it needs, maturity level, risks, best-for scenarios, and how to onboard.

## Key Points

- Three AI types framed by role metaphor: Traditional AI = 'The Analyst,' Generative AI = 'The Creator,' Agentic AI = 'The Worker'
- Maturity ratings differ sharply: Traditional AI is 'High' maturity/widely deployed, Generative AI is 'Medium'/fast-improving but governance still maturing, Agentic AI is 'Early'/powerful but needs strong controls
- Agentic AI's stated risk is explicitly about error cascades requiring 'strict guardrails, approvals and rollback controls' — distinct risk profile from the other two types
- Onboarding guidance differs by type: Traditional AI starts with one prediction problem and clean data; Agentic AI starts with one narrow, high-value workflow with humans kept in the loop
- 'What it needs' column ties directly to infrastructure requirements: Agentic AI needs secure system access, tool permissions, short-term memory, and audit logs

## Why Yaz Saved This

A clear maturity-and-risk-calibrated framework for explaining the three AI types to Bechtel stakeholders — the onboarding guidance per type gives Yaz concrete starting-point advice for scoping pilots at each maturity level.
