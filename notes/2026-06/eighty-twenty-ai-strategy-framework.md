# The 80/20 AI Strategy Framework: Seven Moves That Drive Real AI Value

## Summary

Carolyn Healey's framework cites PwC's 2026 AI predictions that only ~20% of AI value comes from technology while ~80% comes from how work is redesigned — yet most enterprises spend 80% of their energy on tools/pilots and only 20% asking how work should change. Seven moves are listed: start with the workflow (McKinsey: workflow redesign is the strongest predictor of impact); stop confusing adoption with impact (BCG: 74% of companies see no tangible value from AI); kill the pilot, design deployment (MIT: ~95% of generative AI pilots fail to deliver measurable ROI); make your CFO your co-architect (Gartner: AI spend projected to hit $2.52T in 2026); redesign roles before automating tasks (BCG's 10-20-70 rule); treat governance as a scaling lever, not a brake (Gartner predicts delegated execution authority); and measure business outcomes, not AI metrics (McKinsey: only ~6% of companies qualify as AI high performers).

## Key Points

- PwC 2026 prediction cited: ~20% of AI value from technology, ~80% from how work is redesigned — enterprises have the ratio reversed in practice
- MIT research cited: approximately 95% of generative AI pilots fail to deliver measurable ROI, typically because pilots work in controlled settings but collapse in real workflows with messy data
- Gartner-cited AI spend figure: projected to hit $2.52 trillion in 2026, with over half going to infrastructure
- McKinsey-cited stat: only about 6% of companies qualify as AI high performers where AI meaningfully contributes to EBIT
- Move 6 explicitly reframes governance as a 'scaling lever, not a brake' — high performers use delegated decision rights and escalation rules to increase speed and autonomy, not to slow things down
- Closing 'Truth': the 6% of companies generating real AI returns got there by redesigning how their businesses operate, treating technology as the enabler rather than the strategy
