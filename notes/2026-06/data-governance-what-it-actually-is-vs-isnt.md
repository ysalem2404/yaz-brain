# Data Governance: What It Actually Is vs What It Isn't (Whiteboard)

## Summary

John Wernfeldt's whiteboard directly contrasts eight misconceptions ('what people think it is': policies, committees, documentation, compliance checkbox, data dictionary, approval workflows, IT-owned control, stewardship meetings) against eight functional realities ('what it actually is': decision rights, named ownership, metric definitions in code, risk management, shared understanding, escalation clarity, business-owned accountability, operating rhythm). A summary table contrasts 'old version' (focus on process, measures activities, creates overhead, owned by IT, people avoid it) against 'real version' (focus on decisions, measures outcomes, removes friction, owned by business, people ask for it), closing with 'Governance is not a burden you add to the business. It is how the business makes decisions it can trust.'

## Key Points

- Eight-item misconception-vs-reality mapping: 'Policies' → 'Decision rights,' 'Committees' → 'Named ownership,' 'Documentation' → 'Metric definitions in code,' 'Compliance checkbox' → 'Risk management,' 'Data dictionary' → 'Shared understanding,' 'Approval workflows' → 'Escalation clarity,' 'IT-owned control' → 'Business-owned accountability,' 'Stewardship meetings' → 'Operating rhythm'
- Old-vs-real version table: focus on process vs decisions, measures activities vs outcomes, creates overhead vs removes friction, owned by IT vs owned by business, people avoid it vs people ask for it
- Diagnostic rule stated explicitly: 'If people are avoiding your governance program, you built the left column. Start over with the right'
- Closing philosophy: governance is not overhead, it's how the business makes trustworthy decisions
