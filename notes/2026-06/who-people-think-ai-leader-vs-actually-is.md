# Who People Think an AI Leader Is vs. Who an AI Leader Actually Is

## Summary

Stephanie Hills's dual mind-map contrasts perceived versus actual AI leadership traits. Perceived traits: knows the most about AI, first to try every new tool, always talking about AI, automates everything possible, pretends to have all the answers. Actual traits: sets clear direction for AI, focuses on outcomes not tools, asks which problems need AI, protects team from AI overwhelm, trains teams before disruption, prioritizes human judgment, leads calmly through uncertainty, makes AI accessible to everyone.

## Key Points

- Perceived AI leader traits center on personal technical mastery and constant tool experimentation — knows the most, first to try every tool, always talking about AI
- Actual AI leader traits center on direction-setting, prioritization, and team protection — sets clear direction, asks which problems need AI, protects team from AI overwhelm
- 'Protects team from AI overwhelm' and 'trains teams before disruption' explicitly frame AI leadership as a change-management responsibility, not a technical one
- 'Prioritizes human judgment' is listed as a core actual-leader trait, directly countering the perceived trait of 'automates everything possible'
- Promotional footer links to a free AI leadership masterclass (stephanieshills.com/ai)

A concise leadership self-check Yaz can use to calibrate his own approach as an enterprise AI architect at Bechtel — emphasizing outcome focus and team protection over tool obsession, relevant to his forward-deployed engineer and governance work.
