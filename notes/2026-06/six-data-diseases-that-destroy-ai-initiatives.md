# The 6 Data Diseases That Destroy AI Initiatives

## Summary

aidigital.co.uk's diagnostic infographic names six 'data diseases' that undermine AI initiatives, each with symptoms, AI consequence, and treatment: Fragmented Data Syndrome (isolated teams, conflicting reports → AI receives incomplete business context → treat with data integration and a unified platform); Dirty Data Disorder (duplicates, missing/incorrect/outdated data → garbage in, garbage out → treat with quality monitoring and cleansing); Ownership Deficiency (nobody owns key datasets → trust in AI disappears quickly → assign data stewards and owners); Dark Knowledge Syndrome (knowledge trapped in PDFs/emails/chat history → AI misses most enterprise knowledge → knowledge management and retrieval systems); Governance Blindness (uncontrolled access, inconsistent definitions → scaling AI becomes a business risk → policies, standards, governance controls); and Traceability Loss (unknown sources, missing audit trails → AI decisions cannot be explained → data lineage and observability). Includes a self-scoring 'AI Readiness Scorecard' (24-30 = AI Ready, 18-23 = Needs Improvement, below 18 = Fix Data Before AI).

## Key Points

- Six 'diseases,' each mapped to symptoms, a specific AI consequence, and a named treatment — a diagnostic rather than purely descriptive framework
- Dark Knowledge Syndrome explicitly calls out knowledge 'trapped in PDFs, buried in emails, chat history never reused' as a distinct failure mode from general dirty data — directly relevant to enterprise knowledge-base/RAG scoping
- Self-scoring AI Readiness Scorecard rates six areas (data integration, quality, ownership, knowledge accessibility, governance controls, lineage) from 1-5, with thresholds: 24-30 AI Ready, 18-23 Needs Improvement, below 18 Fix Data Before AI
- Traceability Loss disease is treated with 'data lineage and observability' — directly tying explainability failures back to a data engineering root cause rather than a model problem
- Enterprise AI Health Check includes six yes/no readiness signals: data is connected, accurate, has owners; knowledge is accessible; governance is enforced; lineage is visible
