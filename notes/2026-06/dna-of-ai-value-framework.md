# The DNA of AI Value: VALUE Framework for Avoiding Expensive AI Distractions

## Summary

Clare Kitching's VALUE acronym framework distills what separates useful AI from expensive distraction into five steps: Validate the opportunity (get clear on the business problem before choosing a tool), Assign ownership (a named business owner accountable for outcomes, decisions, and risks — not just an enthusiastic IT team), Link it to measurable outcomes (define success in business terms like faster decisions or lower risk), Uncover and understand the risks (privacy, security, bias, trust, reputation trade-offs), and Embed it in the business (AI creates value only when built into how work actually gets done).

## Key Points

- VALUE acronym: Validate opportunity, Assign ownership, Link to measurable outcomes, Uncover risks, Embed in the business
- Explicitly warns against IT-team-only ownership — a named business owner must be accountable for outcomes and risks
- Success must be defined in simple business terms (faster decisions, lower risk, productivity, revenue, CX) rather than technical metrics alone
- Risk step frames trade-offs (privacy, security, bias, trust, reputation) as things to manage before scaling, not eliminate entirely
- Final step (embed) ties AI value to workflow change and capability building beyond the initial pilot — echoes the 'don't bolt AI onto old processes' theme seen elsewhere

## Why Yaz Saved This

A compact ownership-and-outcomes checklist Yaz can apply when scoping new AI use cases at Bechtel, reinforcing the discipline of naming an accountable business owner before any pilot starts.
