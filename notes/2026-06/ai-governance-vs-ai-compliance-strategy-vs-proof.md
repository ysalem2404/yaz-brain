# AI Governance vs. AI Compliance: Strategy vs. Proof

## Summary

A dark-themed comparison infographic distinguishes AI Governance (strategic focus, answering 'how should we manage AI?' via policies/standards, AI principles, risk frameworks, accountability, and decision oversight) from AI Compliance (operational focus, answering 'are we meeting requirements?' via regulations, data privacy, security controls, audits, and documentation). It uses a roadmap analogy — governance creates the map, compliance ensures you follow the rules of the road — and concludes that strong governance without compliance risks regulatory/reputational exposure, while compliance without governance risks unmanaged risk and missed opportunity; mature organizations build both.

## Key Points

- AI Governance answers 'how should we manage AI?' (strategy); AI Compliance answers 'are we meeting requirements?' (operational proof)
- Governance pillars: policies & standards, AI principles, risk frameworks, accountability, decision oversight
- Compliance pillars: regulations, data privacy, security controls, audits, documentation
- Roadmap analogy: governance = the map/direction, compliance = the traffic rules/validation
- Key takeaway: governance without compliance risks regulatory/reputational exposure; compliance without governance risks unmanaged risk and missed opportunity — mature orgs need both together

## Why Yaz Saved This

A crisp governance-vs-compliance distinction directly useful for Yaz's ISO/IEC 42001 and FedRAMP-adjacent governance framework work — clarifies which artifacts belong under 'strategy' versus 'audit proof' when structuring Bechtel's AI governance documentation.
