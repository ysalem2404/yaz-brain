# Two AI Pilots, Same Model, One Scales One Dies: Seven Contrasts That Decide the Outcome

## Summary

Carolyn Healey's seven-contrast framework explains why identical AI models produce wildly different pilot outcomes. Citing McKinsey 2025 (nearly 9 in 10 organizations use AI in at least one function, but only about 6% capture significant enterprise-level value), it contrasts stalling versus scaling behaviors: workflow redesign beats bolt-on (BCG: ~90% of AI value in future-built firms comes from reshaping processes); outcome metrics beat activity metrics; executive ownership beats IT orphans (BCG: near-universal C-suite use is a defining trait of leading firms); tight scope beats boil-the-ocean (Gartner: unclear business value drives 30%+ of GenAI projects abandoned after proof of concept); AI-ready data beats demo data (Gartner: poor data quality is a leading cause of GenAI abandonment); workforce enablement beats tool drops (BCG); and governance as enabler beats governance as afterthought (McKinsey: disciplined risk controls separate value-capturers from the rest).

## Key Points

- McKinsey 2025 stat: nearly 9 in 10 organizations use AI in at least one function, but only about 6% capture significant enterprise-level value — a stark adoption-versus-value gap
- BCG 2025 stat: in future-built firms, nearly 90% of AI value comes from reshaping and reinventing processes, not automating old ones
- Gartner 2025 stat: unclear business value and escalating costs drive at least 30% of GenAI projects to be abandoned after proof of concept
- Governance contrast explicitly reframes governance as an enabler when 'designed in from day one' with human-in-the-loop, versus an afterthought that freezes projects when 'bolted on late'
- Closing insight: 'the model rarely decides the outcome. The operating model does' — reframing the key diagnostic question from 'did we pick the right tool' to 'did we change how the work actually gets done'
