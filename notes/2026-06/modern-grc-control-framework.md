# Modern GRC Control Framework: Governance, Risk, Compliance and Assurance

## Summary

Excellog.Biz's comprehensive GRC framework defines modern GRC across governance (decision rights, policy, accountable ownership), integrated assurance (controls/obligations/risk signals/evidence working together), and accountability layers (board oversight, management ownership, control accountability). It maps the obligation-control-evidence flow, plots control confidence versus exposure (evidence maturity reduces exposure), lists current GRC pressure points (AI governance, third-party risk, cyber resilience, data privacy, regulatory change, sustainability reporting, audit evidence), assessment methods, response actions (strengthen, automate, escalate), core frameworks (COSO IC, ISO 31000, NIST CSF 2.0, COBIT), and popular focus areas (AI governance, privacy compliance, digital resilience, third-party oversight).

## Key Points

- Defines the obligation-control-evidence chain explicitly: Obligation (law/regulation/contract/policy) → Control (designed activity that prevents/detects/corrects exposure) → Evidence (proof the control exists, operates, and is reviewed)
- Control Confidence vs Exposure chart shows exposure decreasing as evidence maturity increases — a visual argument for investing in evidence discipline, not just control design
- AI Governance is listed as one of seven 'current GRC pressure points' alongside third-party risk, cyber resilience, data privacy, regulatory change, sustainability reporting, and audit evidence
- Four core GRC frameworks named: COSO IC (internal control/governance assurance), ISO 31000 (risk management principles/guidance), NIST CSF 2.0 (cybersecurity governance/resilience), COBIT (IT governance)
- Closing principle: 'Better GRC is not more paperwork. It is clearer ownership, stronger evidence and faster risk decisions'

## Why Yaz Saved This

A comprehensive GRC reference that explicitly names AI Governance as a top-tier pressure point within broader enterprise risk/compliance frameworks — useful for positioning Bechtel's AI governance work within existing COSO/NIST CSF/COBIT-aligned GRC programs.
