# ISO 42001 + EU AI Act: The Compliance Bridge Every Organization Needs

## Summary

Aakash Yadav's infographic maps ISO 42001 clauses directly to EU AI Act articles across six control areas — risk management, documentation, data governance, human oversight, performance monitoring, and continuous improvement — showing both frameworks require the same underlying practices (e.g., risk management: ISO 42001 Clause 6.1 maps to EU AI Act Article 9; both require identifying AI risks, assessing impact, applying controls, and reviewing across the AI lifecycle). A three-phase, six-month implementation roadmap is given: Phase 1 (months 1-2) gap assessment and AI policy creation, Phase 2 (months 3-4) implementing controls and documentation, Phase 3 (months 5-6) internal audits and certification prep. Key takeaway: ISO 42001 doesn't replace the EU AI Act but gives organizations a practical operating system for AI compliance.

## Key Points

- Six-area core mapping table: Risk Management (ISO 42001 Clause 6.1 ↔ EU AI Act Article 9), Documentation (Clause 7.5 ↔ Article 11), Data Governance (Annex B ↔ Article 10), Human Oversight (Clause 8.4 ↔ Article 14), Performance Monitoring (Clause 9 ↔ Article 15), Continuous Improvement (Clause 10 ↔ Article 72)
- Three-phase, six-month implementation roadmap: gap assessment/policy (months 1-2) → controls/documentation (months 3-4) → audits/certification (months 5-6)
- Key takeaway explicitly stated: 'ISO 42001 does not replace the EU AI Act' but provides the practical operating system connecting risk, documentation, oversight, and improvement
- ISO 42001 is called 'the world's first AI management system standard' and works well alongside existing ISO 27001 programs
- Frames ISO 42001 as giving AI governance a clear PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act) structure with audit-ready evidence for compliance teams
