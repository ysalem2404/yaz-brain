# Types of AI: Which One Actually Solves Your Problem?

## Summary

Clare Kitching's decision-oriented taxonomy breaks 'Types of AI' into five categories with concrete example uses for each: AI Agents & Agentic AI (plans, decides, takes action — end-to-end workflow execution, automated service resolution, multi-step booking); Generative AI (creates content and summaries — drafting content/code, summarizing documents, customer support chat); Deep Learning (learns complex patterns automatically — image/video analysis, speech recognition, defect detection); Classical ML/Predictive AI (forecasts outcomes from past data — demand forecasting, churn prediction, credit scoring); and Rules & Logic (if X happens do Y — compliance checks, eligibility screening, approval routing).

## Key Points

- Five AI categories framed around 'which one actually solves your problem' rather than technical sophistication
- AI Agents & Agentic AI category explicitly separated from Generative AI — action-taking versus content-creation
- Rules & Logic (deterministic if-X-then-Y systems) is explicitly included as a legitimate 'type of AI' for compliance checks and threshold-based fraud flags — a reminder that not every automation problem needs an LLM
- Classical ML/Predictive AI examples (demand forecasting, churn prediction, credit scoring, predictive maintenance, price optimization) are the most business-familiar and often the best ROI starting point
- Deep Learning examples focus on perception tasks: image/video analysis, speech recognition, OCR, recommendation engines

## Why Yaz Saved This

A practical decision framework for matching problem type to AI category — useful for Yaz when scoping which Bechtel use cases genuinely need GenAI/agentic AI versus simpler rules-based or classical ML approaches, avoiding over-engineering.
