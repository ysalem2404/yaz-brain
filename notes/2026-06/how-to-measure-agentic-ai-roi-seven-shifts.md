# How to Measure Agentic AI ROI: 7 Metrics Shifts for the Next Board Meeting

## Summary

Carolyn Healey's infographic cites PwC's 2026 Global CEO Survey finding that 56% of CEOs say their AI investment has not produced meaningful revenue or cost benefit, and only 12% can point to both. It argues the gap isn't talent or technology — it's what gets measured. Seven metric shifts are proposed: stop reporting adoption, start reporting outcomes; pick one measurable outcome metric per agent; treat token spend as a visible cost line item (acknowledging compute, retrieval, orchestration, monitoring, and failure recovery all add cost); build the guardrail/harness budget into the business case; set a human-only baseline before deployment; watch foundation metrics (data quality, governance maturity, integration depth) that predict scalability; and retire vanity benchmarks like generic 'hours saved' claims.

## Key Points

- PwC 2026 Global CEO Survey: 56% of CEOs report no meaningful revenue/cost benefit from AI investment; only 12% can show both
- Core critique: usage/adoption percentage is not an ROI metric — it only matters when tied to cost reduction, revenue increase, margin improvement, or risk reduction
- Recommends one named, measurable outcome metric per agent (cost per resolved ticket, qualified lead, contract reviewed, incident closed)
- Token spend must be treated as a full-cost line item including compute, retrieval, orchestration, monitoring, and exception handling — not just headline API cost
- Guardrail/harness costs (evals, monitoring, permissions, audit trails, human review) should be built into the business case, not treated as overhead
- Recommends capturing a human-only process baseline (cycle time, error rate, cost per task) before deployment so improvement claims are measurable, not anecdotal

## Why Yaz Saved This

A board-ready ROI framework with a hard PwC statistic (56% no benefit) Yaz can use to push for rigorous baseline-and-outcome measurement before Bechtel commits further budget to agentic AI pilots.
