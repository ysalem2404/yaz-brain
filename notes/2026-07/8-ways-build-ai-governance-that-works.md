# 8 Ways to Build AI Governance That Actually Works

## Summary

An 8-pillar operational AI governance checklist: AI Inventory & Ownership (shadow AI detection, risk tiering), Data Foundation (lineage, provenance, freshness monitoring), Model Lifecycle (registry, bias testing, drift monitoring, retrain/rollback), Data Security & Privacy (encryption, PII anonymization, injection defense), Access Control (RBAC, least privilege), Agent Governance (tool permissions, autonomy limits, kill switch), Human Oversight (decision review, escalation paths, explainability), and Compliance & Audit (regulatory mapping, incident reporting, audit trails).

## Key Points

- AI Inventory & Ownership: shadow AI detection, system classification, risk tiering, vendor/model mapping
- Data Foundation: source lineage, data provenance, quality validation, licensing and consent tracking
- Model Lifecycle: model registry, pre-deploy evaluation, bias/fairness testing, drift monitoring, retrain/rollback
- Agent Governance: tool permissions, action boundaries, autonomy limits, human-in-the-loop, kill switch
- Human Oversight: decision review, escalation paths, output validation, explainability, accountability mapping
- Compliance & Audit: regulatory mapping, privacy alignment, model documentation, incident reporting, audit trails

## Why Yaz Saved This

A granular, checklist-level AI governance operating model — the 'kill switch' and 'autonomy limits' items under Agent Governance are especially relevant for Bechtel's FedRAMP/GCCH agentic AI risk controls.
