# How to Explain Data Governance: The Four-Ring Operating Model

## Summary

A whiteboard-style diagram by John Wernfeldt of Northridge Analytics frames data governance as a set of four concentric rings — Data Foundation, Governance, Trusted Analytics, and AI Ready — arguing governance is an operating model, not a policy folder. The innermost ring covers foundational artifacts (data catalog, data quality, metric dictionary, ownership register); each outer ring depends entirely on the ring inside it, with a sticky note warning 'you cannot buy the outer ring without building every ring inside it first.' The four quadrants (Concepts, Use Cases, Artifacts, Roles) map each ring to concrete deliverables like model cards, KPI catalogs, semantic layers, and roles such as CDO, DPO, Data Steward, and AI Governance Analyst. The outermost ring ties directly to AI readiness and the EU AI Act.

## Key Points

- Four concentric rings: Data Foundation (know what you have) → Governance (operating model) → Trusted Analytics (numbers you can defend) → AI Ready (strong enough for AI)
- Central warning: 'Start here. Everything else depends on this' — data catalog, data quality, metric dictionary, and ownership register are the non-negotiable foundation
- You cannot buy or skip to the outer AI-ready ring without building every inner ring first — a direct rebuttal to 'just buy an AI governance tool' thinking
- Roles quadrant names specific accountable owners: Data Steward, Data Owner, Analytics Lead, DPO, CDO, AI Gov Analyst, AI Gov Board
- Artifacts quadrant lists concrete deliverables: data catalog, lineage, model cards, KPI catalog, use-case inventory, certified reports
- Outer ring explicitly ties governance maturity to EU AI Act compliance and responsible-AI use cases
