# AI vs ML vs DL vs GenAI vs LLMs vs RAG vs Agentic AI: The Hierarchy Explained

## Summary

This nesting-umbrella infographic from AI For Leaders lays out the hierarchy from AI down through ML, DL, GenAI, LLMs, RAG, and Agentic AI, framing them as related-but-not-interchangeable layers that build on each other rather than synonyms. Each layer gets a definition, a one-line description, and concrete named examples (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini for GenAI/LLMs; enterprise chatbots and document Q&A for RAG; OpenAI Agents, Claude Computer Use, AutoGPT, LangGraph for Agentic AI). A simple evolution flow and a real-world-examples table map each layer to concrete use cases like spam detection, self-driving cars, and workflow automation. The closing panel lists 'key modern concepts to know' — multimodal AI, fine-tuning, prompt engineering, AI safety, and AI agents — as the vocabulary leaders need going into 2026.

## Key Points

- AI is the broad umbrella; ML, DL, GenAI, LLMs, RAG, and Agentic AI are progressively narrower subsets, not synonyms
- Evolution flow: AI (think/reason) → ML (learn patterns) → DL (complex patterns) → GenAI (create content) → LLMs (understand/generate language) → RAG (knowledge + GenAI) → Agentic AI (act autonomously toward goals)
- RAG is explicitly framed as combining GenAI with external knowledge for accurate, up-to-date answers — directly relevant to enterprise chatbot and knowledge-base design
- Agentic AI examples cited include OpenAI Agents, Claude Computer Use, AutoGPT, LangGraph Agents, and Manus AI
- Five reasons the hierarchy matters: choosing the right approach, building smarter products, using RAG/agents correctly for accuracy and autonomy, and staying future-ready
- Closing glossary of 'key modern concepts' (multimodal AI, fine-tuning, prompt engineering, AI safety, AI agents) doubles as a quick executive-briefing cheat sheet
