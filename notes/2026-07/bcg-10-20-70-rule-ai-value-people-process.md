# 70% of AI's Value Comes From People and Process: BCG's 10-20-70 Rule

## Summary

This infographic unpacks BCG's 10-20-70 rule — AI success is driven 10% by algorithms, 20% by tech and data, and 70% by people and processes — and argues enterprise AI budgets still skew disproportionately toward technology, missing the bigger lever. A seven-point 'the 70% playbook' covers workflow redesign as the single best ROI predictor, why bolting AI onto old processes wastes money, why adoption is a leadership behavior rather than a training exercise, why agents raise the stakes on ownership and human design, why concentration beats sprawl in use-case selection, and why leaders should measure business transformation (EBIT, cycle time, revenue) rather than the tool itself. It cites McKinsey data that only 39% of organizations report enterprise-level EBIT impact from AI.

## Key Points

- BCG's 10-20-70 rule: 10% algorithms, 20% tech/data, 70% people and process drive AI success
- McKinsey research cited: redesigning workflows is one of the strongest contributors to AI business impact; only 39% of orgs report enterprise-level EBIT impact from AI
- Bolting AI onto unchanged processes is called 'the most expensive way to get nothing' — the inefficiency being automated doesn't go away
- Adoption is framed as a leadership behavior (role-modeling ownership) rather than a one-time training/enablement session
- Agentic AI raises the stakes on the 70% because autonomous agents change how work gets done and who owns the outcome
- Recommendation: concentrate AI investment on fewer, deeper use cases rather than spreading effort thin across many pilots

## Why Yaz Saved This

Strong evidence-based counter to tech-only thinking that Yaz can cite when pushing Bechtel leadership to invest in workflow redesign and change management alongside any AI tooling purchase, not just the platform itself.
