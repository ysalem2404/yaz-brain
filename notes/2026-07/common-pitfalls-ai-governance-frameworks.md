# Common Pitfalls in AI Governance Frameworks

## Summary

An 8-pitfall infographic on AI governance failure patterns: governance designed in isolation, no clear ownership, one-time governance approach, compliance-only mindset, poor risk classification, shadow AI usage, limited post-deployment monitoring, and weak human oversight. It contrasts these with a 'better approach': risk-based, continuously monitored, cross-functionally owned, embedded in workflows, supported by human oversight, and practical/adoptable.

## Key Points

- Pitfall: governance created without business/product/engineering/security involvement
- Pitfall: no clear accountability for models, decisions, performance, or incident response
- Pitfall: frameworks created once and not updated as models/data/use cases evolve
- Pitfall: compliance-only mindset ignores reliability, transparency, ethics, and business value
- Pitfall: shadow AI usage from unapproved tools causes data leakage and lost control
- Better approach: risk-based, continuously monitored, cross-functional ownership, embedded in workflows, human oversight, practical and adoptable
- Core message: governed AI scales, ungoverned AI creates risk

## Why Yaz Saved This

A useful self-check list for auditing whether Bechtel's own emerging AI governance program is falling into common traps like one-time policy creation or compliance-only thinking, especially around shadow AI detection relevant to FedRAMP/GCCH environments.
