# AI Governance vs Data Governance — Why You Need Both

## Summary

A Carolyn Healey infographic contrasting Data Governance and AI Governance across eight dimensions — what they govern, risks they answer, controls they use, ownership, operating cadence, regulatory pressure, inputs vs outputs, and dependency — framed around the question 'why did your AI flag that customer?' It cites Gartner's prediction that through 2026, organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects unsupported by AI-ready data, and notes organizations with successful AI initiatives invest up to 4x more in foundations like data quality, governance, AI-ready people, and change management. The conclusion: if one team blames the data and another blames the model, the organization doesn't have a governance problem in one discipline — it has an integration problem across both.

## Key Points

- Eight-dimension comparison: what they govern, risks addressed, controls used, ownership, operating cadence, regulatory pressure, inputs vs outputs, mutual dependency
- Gartner: through 2026, organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects unsupported by AI-ready data
- Successful AI initiatives invest up to 4x more in governance/data-quality/people foundations than others
- Data governance gives AI 'clean, traceable, authorized inputs'; AI governance makes outputs 'explainable, monitored, and accountable'
- AI governance explicitly tied to EU AI Act tiered penalties and obligations for high-risk systems
- Core thesis: data governance and AI governance failures are often mislabeled as single-discipline problems when they're really integration failures

Reinforces and sharpens the data-governance-to-AI-governance mapping seen elsewhere in this batch — the 60% project abandonment statistic and 4x investment finding are strong, citable data points for Yaz's governance business case conversations.
