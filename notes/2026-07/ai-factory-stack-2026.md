# The AI Factory Stack: How Modern AI Systems Produce Real-World Results in 2026

## Summary

Brij Kishore Pandey's factory-line analogy maps each AI system component to a familiar manufacturing role: the LLM is 'the machine' generating text and reasoning; RAG is the 'raw material room' finding the right information; the Vector DB is the 'storage warehouse' retrieving by meaning; the AI Agent is the 'floor manager' deciding what to do next and using tools; MCP is the 'power outlet standard' connecting agents to tools/apps/APIs/files/databases with one standard; Guardrails are the 'safety system' defining what AI should and shouldn't do; and Evals are the 'quality check' testing correctness, safety, cost, latency, and success. The closing line: 'Great AI isn't just a powerful model. It's a well-run factory.'

## Key Points

- Seven-component stack mapped to factory roles: LLM=machine, RAG=raw material room, Vector DB=storage warehouse, AI Agent=floor manager, MCP=power outlet standard, Guardrails=safety system, Evals=quality check
- MCP explicitly framed as 'one standard, many connections' for agent-to-tool/app/API/file/database integration
- Guardrails defined narrowly as protecting users, data, and actions by defining allowed/disallowed AI behavior
- Evals measure quality, cost, latency, and success — not just correctness — as the factory's quality-check station
- Core thesis: production AI value comes from the full pipeline working together, not from model power alone
