# The $7 Trillion AI Data Center Buildout: 2030 CAPEX Breakdown

![Infographic](public/img/notes/2026-05/7-trillion-ai-data-center-buildout-2030.png)

## Summary
Illustrative breakdown of a projected $7.0T global AI data center CAPEX by 2030, based on a McKinsey estimate cited by KKR. Accelerated Compute leads at $2.45T (35%) benefiting Nvidia/AMD/TSMC/SK hynix/Micron; Power + Grid is $1.40T (20%) benefiting Eaton/Schneider Electric/ABB/Siemens/GE Vernova; Construction + Land $1.05T (15%); Networking + Optics $0.84T (12%); Cooling + Thermal $0.56T (8%); Storage + Memory $0.35T (5%); Security + Orchestration $0.21T (3%, benefiting Palo Alto Networks/CrowdStrike/Zscaler/Cloudflare/Okta); Services + O&M $0.14T (2%). Key takeaway: 'compute is the largest pool, but power is the bottleneck,' and the 'less crowded winners sit downstream in power, cooling, optics, and grid equipment' where 'land, permits, and power access create durable moats.'

## Key points
- Compute (35%) is the biggest CAPEX pool, but power/grid access is called the actual bottleneck
- Security + Orchestration is the smallest named category at just 3% ($0.21T) of total buildout
- Downstream/less-crowded winners are power, cooling, optics, and grid equipment vendors — not just chipmakers
- Land, permits, and power access are framed as the durable competitive moat, not compute supply
- Source is explicitly caveated as 'illustrative' — a McKinsey estimate via KKR, not a hard forecast

## Entities & tools mentioned
