# The Executive's Guide to Types of AI (5-Tier Risk/Capability Ladder)

![Infographic](public/img/notes/2026-05/executives-guide-types-of-ai-5-tier.png)

## Summary
A 5-tier executive taxonomy of AI types ordered from simplest to most capable/risky: Rules & Logic ('if X happens, do Y' — brittle at scale, use when you need certainty/auditability), Classical ML/Predictive AI (forecasts from historical data — 'proven use cases with high ROI, less exciting than GenAI but more reliable'), Deep Learning (learns complex patterns, powerful but expensive, low explainability, 'overkill for simple problems'), Generative AI (creates content/summaries — 'treat it as an assistant, not an authority' due to hallucination/IP-leakage risk), and AI Agents/Agentic AI (plans, decides, takes actions — 'where value compounds and risk multiplies... constrain tightly, scale carefully'). Each tier lists use cases, technical requirements, and key risks.

## Key points
- Explicit risk gradient: risk and value both compound as you move from rules-based to agentic AI
- Generative AI's core risk framing: 'treat it as an assistant, not an authority' — a governance-friendly one-liner
- Agentic AI's guidance is 'constrain tightly, scale carefully' — not 'avoid,' but bounded rollout
- Classical ML is explicitly defended as more reliable and higher-ROI than flashier GenAI/agentic approaches
- Each tier pairs use cases with 'what it needs' (data, compute, human oversight) — useful for build-vs-buy sizing
