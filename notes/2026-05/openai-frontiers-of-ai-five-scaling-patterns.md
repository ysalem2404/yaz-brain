# OpenAI Frontiers of AI: Five Patterns That Let European Enterprises Scale AI Past Pilots

![Infographic](public/img/notes/2026-05/openai-frontiers-of-ai-five-scaling-patterns.pdf)

## Summary
This OpenAI "Frontiers of AI" report (April 2026) synthesizes interviews with six European enterprise leaders — Philips, BBVA, Mirakl, Scout24, JetBrains, and Scania — on what separated durable AI scale-ups from stalled pilots, describing the core obstacle as a "capability gap: what AI can do is moving ahead of what most organizations can actually deploy." It identifies five consistent patterns: culture before tooling (Philips trained senior leaders first across a 70,000-person workforce), governance as an enabler rather than a blocker (BBVA embedded security/legal/compliance from the outset and cut query handling time from 7.5 minutes to about one minute for 3,000+ employees before scaling ChatGPT Enterprise to 120,000+), ownership over consumption (Mirakl let employees build their own agents, driving 70% faster documentation and 91% faster catalog onboarding), quality before scale (Scout24 built custom eval frameworks inspired by OpenAI Evals and delayed launches that missed quality bars), and protecting judgment work (JetBrains used AI to support, not replace, expert code review and system design).

## Key points
- Core framing: a 'capability gap' between what AI can do and what most organizations can actually deploy in production
- Pattern 1 (Philips): culture before tooling — trained senior leaders first across a 70,000-employee healthcare/tech workforce
- Pattern 2 (BBVA): governance as an enabler — security/legal/compliance embedded from the outset; internal assistant cut query time from ~7.5 min to ~1 min for 3,000+ employees before ChatGPT Enterprise scaled to 120,000+
- Pattern 3 (Mirakl): ownership over consumption — employee-built agents drove 70% faster documentation, 91% faster catalog onboarding at half the error rate, 37% support efficiency gain at 96% satisfaction
- Pattern 4 (Scout24): quality before scale — custom eval frameworks inspired by OpenAI Evals, features delayed until quality thresholds met
- Pattern 5 (JetBrains): protecting judgment work — AI framed as support for review/reasoning/system design rather than a replacement for developer judgment
