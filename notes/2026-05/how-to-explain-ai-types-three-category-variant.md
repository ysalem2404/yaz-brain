# How to Explain AI Types: Traditional, Generative, and Agentic AI (3-category variant)

## Summary

Aditya Sharma's three-category taxonomy (distinct from the four-category Predictive/Generative/Agentic/Physical AI frameworks captured elsewhere) organizes AI into Traditional AI (predictive analytics, classification systems, anomaly detection), Generative AI (content generation, workflow automation, knowledge systems/RAG), and Agentic AI (AI agents & tool use, multi-agent orchestration, AI product integration) — each with three sub-capabilities and brief examples.

## Key Points

- Traditional AI: predictive analytics (forecast demand/risk), classification systems (sort/route data), anomaly detection (fraud, system failures, security breaches)
- Generative AI: content generation (drafts, code, designs from prompts), workflow automation (email sorting, meeting notes, data cleaning), knowledge systems/RAG
- Agentic AI: AI agents & tool use (execute tasks via APIs/tools), multi-agent orchestration (delegate and coordinate), AI product integration
- Uses only 3 top-level categories (no separate 'Physical AI' tier), unlike the 4-category frameworks from AI For Leaders and Jason Moccia captured in batches 7-8
- Positioned as a LinkedIn-native explainer format with a 'save for later' bookmark call-to-action
