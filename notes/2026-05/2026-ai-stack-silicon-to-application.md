# The 2026 AI Stack: From Silicon to Application (5 Layers and the Stack Race)

## Summary

A five-layer stack argues that the AI competition has shifted from 'what model is smartest' to 'who controls the stack': Layer 1 Chips & Hardware (NVIDIA, AMD, TPU, custom ASICs — the foundational bottleneck), Layer 2 Cloud & Infrastructure (AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, CoreWeave — massive capex, high barriers to entry), Layer 3 Foundational & Small Models (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta Llama, Mistral, Cohere, Hugging Face — the differentiation/arms-race layer), Layer 4 Orchestration & Frameworks (LangChain, LlamaIndex, AutoGen, CrewAI, Relevance AI, Vertex AI — enables scale and creates stickiness/moats), and Layer 5 Application & UI (ChatGPT, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Claude, Perplexity, Midjourney, Canva, Notion — top of the funnel, high multiples for defensible products). It closes noting that Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and NVIDIA are each trying to own multiple layers simultaneously.

## Key Points

- 5 layers bottom-up: Chips & Hardware, Cloud & Infrastructure, Foundational & Small Models, Orchestration & Frameworks, Application & UI
- Layer 1 (chips) is called the foundational bottleneck — performance per watt and supply dictate the pace of AI
- Layer 3 (models) is described as where the arms race lives — differentiation through capabilities, training data, and model quality
- Layer 4 (orchestration) creates powerful moats and stickiness through workflow orchestration, agent platforms, and tooling
- Big tech strategy comparison: Google (models/TPUs/cloud/search/workspace), Microsoft (OpenAI partnership/Azure/Copilot), Amazon (AWS/Bedrock), Meta (open models/distribution), NVIDIA (silicon/CUDA ecosystem)
- Core thesis: 'control the stack, control the economics, control the ecosystem' — companies are racing to own multiple layers, not just compete at one
