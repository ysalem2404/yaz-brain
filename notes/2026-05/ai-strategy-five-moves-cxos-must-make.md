# AI Strategy That Delivers Real Value: 5 Moves CXOs Must Make

## Summary

Carolyn Healey's CXO strategy guide argues that AI ambition without organizational readiness is 'a leak in value,' rejecting Gartner's 'enablement illusion' (assuming tool access equals AI-enabled organization) in favor of five concrete moves: make the 'why' specific (vague transformation language creates skepticism), address the fear directly (silence about job impact creates resistance), train by role not by tool (generic training has limited value), redesign the workflow (not just bolt AI onto unchanged processes), and measure adoption not access (track usage, behavior change, and business impact, not just who has licenses). It also maps required executive alignment across CEO, CFO, COO, CHRO, and CMO roles.

## Key Points

- Rejects Gartner's 'enablement illusion': having AI tools does not mean the organization is AI-enabled
- 5 moves: make the 'why' specific, address the fear directly, train by role not by tool, redesign the workflow, measure adoption not access
- 3 levels of AI measurement: usage (who/how often), behavior change (are workflows/decisions actually changing), business impact (cost/speed/quality/revenue/CX)
- AI requires executive alignment across CEO (sponsorship), CFO (financial discipline), COO (operating model changes), CHRO (workforce strategy/trust), CMO (customer/market alignment)
- Core message: 'AI is a people change, not just a technology change' — treat AI as a change program, not a procurement decision

## Why Yaz Saved This

Directly relevant CXO-level framing for Yaz's enterprise AI architecture role — reinforces that governance, training, and workflow redesign matter as much as the technology itself when advising Bechtel leadership.
