# What Is Quantum Computing? Qubits, Superposition, and Research-Stage Applications

## Summary

A companion 'AI Simplified Series' primer (3/5) from NMF.org explaining quantum computing fundamentals: classical bits (0 or 1, one state at a time, sequential processing) versus qubits (probability states, superposition, interference), the four-step process of how quantum computers work (superposition, entanglement, quantum operations, measurement), and six research-stage applications (drug discovery, materials science, cryptography, quantum chemistry, climate modeling, cybersecurity).

## Key Points

- Classical bits: 0 or 1, one possibility at a time, sequential processing
- Qubits: probability amplitudes for 0 and 1 held in superposition, using interference to amplify correct answers
- Four-step process: superposition -> entanglement -> quantum operations (gates) -> measurement (collapses to a readable 0/1 result)
- Six research-stage applications: drug discovery, materials science, cryptography (both developing and breaking encryption), quantum chemistry, climate modeling, cybersecurity
- Important caveat: quantum computing is still early-stage; wide practical use is likely years away despite accelerating progress
